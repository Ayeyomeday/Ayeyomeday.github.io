Midterm Reflection: Growing as a Software Engineer

November 5, 2026

As I reflect on the first half of CSC 426, Open Source Software Engineering, I can confidently say that I have learned a lot about software development and what it means to work collaboratively on a project. Before this course, most of my programming experience came from assignments where I worked independently and built programs from scratch. Working on the Berea College Syllabus Repository (BCSR) has introduced me to a different development environment, where understanding existing code, communicating with others, and contributing meaningful improvements are just as important as writing code. One thing that has gone well is my growing familiarity with Git, GitHub, and the open-source development workflow. At the beginning of the semester, I sometimes struggled with understanding branches, commits, pull requests, and how local changes interacted with remote repositories. Although I still encounter difficulties, I have become more comfortable navigating repositories, identifying relevant files, and making changes. I have also enjoyed learning from other contributors and reviewing their work. Being able to test someone else's changes and provide constructive feedback has helped me recognize that software engineering involves collaboration rather than simply completing individual tasks. Another area where I have improved is my understanding of web development. Through BCSR, I have worked with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to address interface issues. For example, I worked on making the syllabus upload area more compact and improving the behavior of deadline description textareas. These tasks required me to understand how different files interact and how seemingly minor changes can affect the user experience. I have learned to approach problems more carefully by examining existing functionality before attempting to modify it. However, not everything has gone as planned. One of my biggest challenges has been navigating unfamiliar codebases and determining where to begin when approaching an issue. Sometimes I spend more time than expected trying to understand the structure of the application or figuring out why a particular solution is not working. I have also encountered difficulties with Git commands and repository management, which occasionally slowed my progress. While some challenges come from the complexity of an existing project, I recognize that improving my preparation and troubleshooting habits is something within my control. For the second half of the semester, one habit I want to improve is how I approach unfamiliar issues before beginning implementation. Instead of immediately trying different solutions, I want to spend more time understanding the problem, identifying the relevant files, and developing a clear plan. I believe this will help me work more efficiently and reduce unnecessary trial and error. My action plan is to begin each issue by reviewing its requirements, exploring the affected code, and writing down the changes I expect to make. After implementing a solution, I will test it carefully and review my changes before committing or submitting a pull request. I also want to continue asking questions and learning from other contributors when necessary.

Progress Update

So far, I have completed 8 story points through my contributions to BCSR. My work has included improving the syllabus upload interface, adjusting the behavior of deadline description textareas, and participating in the review and testing of other contributors' changes. These experiences have helped me better understand the application's structure and the importance of collaboration throughout the development process. Some tasks took longer than I initially anticipated because I needed to familiarize myself with unfamiliar code and understand how different components interacted. However, as I became more comfortable navigating the codebase, I found myself approaching issues with greater confidence. Beyond my completed story points, reviewing pull requests and providing feedback have also been valuable experiences that allowed me to contribute to the project's overall quality. Although I still have room to improve my efficiency, I am satisfied with my progress and look forward to taking on more challenging issues.
