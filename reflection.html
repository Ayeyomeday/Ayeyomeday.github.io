Open Source Reflection #1

October 22, 2026

Throughout this semester in CSC 426, I have learned a lot about what it means to contribute to open-source projects. Before taking this course, I mainly understood open source as software that anyone could access and modify. However, I have realized that it involves much more, including communication, collaboration, and responsibility. Through our readings and my experience contributing to the Berea College Syllabus Repository (BCSR), I have developed a greater appreciation for open-source development. I believe open source can be effective for critical software infrastructure, but only when projects have responsible maintainers, strong communities, and reliable security practices.

One topic that stood out from our readings was the importance of OSS communities and communication. Learning about global communities, the synthetic third culture, and the qualities of successful open-source communities helped me understand how people with different experiences can work toward a common goal. I especially enjoy this aspect of open source because I like learning from others while also providing feedback wherever I can. Seeing how other contributors approach problems has helped me improve my own understanding of software development.

Our readings about development environments, command-line tools, virtualization, and version control also connected directly to our coursework. Working with Git, GitHub, branches, and pull requests has helped me understand how developers organize and collaborate on projects. Although I initially found some Git operations confusing, I have become more comfortable navigating repositories, tracking changes, and understanding how contributions are reviewed before being incorporated into a project. With BCSR specifically, I worked on improving the syllabus upload interface to make the upload area more compact and organized. I also worked on improving deadline description textareas so they would resize appropriately while maintaining the page's layout. These experiences helped me better understand how HTML, CSS, and JavaScript interact and how even small changes can improve the user experience.

I have also enjoyed reviewing other contributors' work. Being able to review peoples work displays contributing does not always require writing code. Reviewing pull requests, testing functionality, and offering constructive feedback are equally valuable parts of the development process. I appreciate having opportunities to learn from others while contributing my own observations.

Another important topic from our readings was open-source licensing. Learning about different licenses and the responsibilities associated with sharing software helped me recognize that making code publicly available does not automatically make a project sustainable or secure. Although open source encourages transparency and collaboration, critical software infrastructure still requires maintenance, testing, and accountability. I also believe organizations that depend on open-source projects should support the contributors who maintain them. Overall, I have genuinely enjoyed learning about open-source software engineering. This course has shown me that software development involves much more than programming. It requires patience, communication, and a willingness to learn. I hope to continue improving my technical skills while contributing to projects, learning from others, and offering meaningful feedback whenever possible.
